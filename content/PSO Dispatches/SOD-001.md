**SALVAGE OFFICER DISPATCH NO. 01**

Seventy-One Days: The _Trigger's_ Human and Steel connection to the _Silversides_

**Vessels:** USS _Silversides_ (SS-236) | USS _Trigger_ (SS-237) | USS _Caiman_ (SS-323)

### I. The Shared Steel

After a long tow across Lake Michigan, _Silversides_ (SS-236) was lifted onto a set of blocks in Sturgeon Bay for her first dry-docking in more than half a century. As the shipbuilders started removing parts for maintenance they discovered something strange in Ballast Tank 2D. The aft flood valve didn't bear _Silversides_ hull number `SS-236`, it was from the _Trigger_ `SS-237`.

As America came to the close of 1941, _Silversides_ and _Trigger_ lay next to each other in the building ways of Mare Island Navy Yard. The first of eight sisters built there. _Silversides_ was commissioned first, on 15 December 1941; _Trigger_ followed her 46 days later. The _Gato_-class was expanding rapidly, and in the mad dash to get boats in the water a flood valve intended for the _Trigger_ was bolted into the ballast tank of her older sister. Nearly 81 years after _Trigger_ was lost with all hands in the East China Sea, a piece of _Trigger_ survived inside the ballast tank of _Silversides._ 

It would be foolish to assume this is the only connection between these two legendary subs. By cross-refereicing the quarterly muster rolls and Reports of Changes (ROCs) against the commissioning and loss musters of the _Gato_-class the human connections between sister ships appears as a mirror of that valve. For exactly seventy-one days in the winter of 1942 to 1943, two Torpedomen Third Class; George Sylvester Hollis Jr. and William Hatfield Rae shared the torpedo rooms of the _Silversides._ One was fresh off of commissioning the Trigger; the other was on a winding journey to her last fateful dive.

### II. Two Lives, Different Coasts

#### 1. The Trigger Plankowner: George Sylvester Hollis Jr., TM1c(SS)

- **Born:** 18 Jan 1921; Brunswick County, NC | **Enlisted:** 7 Jul 1940; Raleigh, NC
- **USS _Trigger_ (SS-237):** Commissioning Plankowner (30 Jan 1942)
- **USS _Silversides_ (SS-236):** Received aboard **10 Jul 1942** - Transferred to USS _Sperry_ (AS-12) **19 Feb 1943** 

George Sylvester Hollis Jr. grew up in the Cape Fear country of Brunswick County, NC; the son of George Sylvester Hollis Sr. and Lillian Blanche Holford. In the spring of 1940, as the local economy clawed its way out of the Depression, George Sr. supported the family working as a bag printer at a local fertilizer plant along the river.

Three Months after the 1940 census was enumerated, on **7 Jul 1940**, George Jr. traveled up to Raleigh and enlisted in the US Navy. By January 1942, the nineteen year old from North Carolina was standing on the deck of the _Trigger_ as a member of the commissioning crew, a plankowner. Just five months later, after _Silversides_ returned to Pearl Harbor from her second war patrol; Hollis transferred aboard. Like the flood valve in MBT # 2D, Hollis was a piece of the _Trigger_ incorporated directly into the torpedo rooms of the _Silversides._ 

#### 2. The Caiman Plankowner: William Hatfield Rae, TM3c(SS)

![[WmRae.jpg]]
- **Born:** Reno, Nevada | **Enlisted:** 14 Oct 1941, San Diego, CA (Also served 3 years in US Army)
- **USS _Silversides_ (SS-236):** Received aboard **10 Dec 1942** - Transferred **27 Sep 1943** 
- **USS _Caiman_ (SS-323):** Commissioning Plankowner (17 Jul 1944)
- **USS _Trigger_ (SS-237):** Received aboard from SubDiv 282 (**22 Feb 1945**) - Lost with all hands (March 1945)

William Hatfield Rae was born in Reno, Nevada under the shadow of an early loss. His father, 2nd Lt. William Andrew Rae (a WWI Marine Corps Reserve officer and chief clerk for Standard Oil), died of a cardiac episode in Maricopa, AZ, at just twenty-eight years old. As his mother, Roxie Alice Hatfield, navigated the Depression as a widowed mother and eventually remarried, both William and his brother Donald turned towards a life of service to their country. Donald Lenore Rae would go on to serve as a Master Sergeant in the Army Air Forces in WWII, continuing his service through Vietnam and his retirement in 1967. 

Before Pearl Harbor, William Rae had already completed a full three-year peacetime hitch in the US Army. Discharged in in the fall of 1941, he registered for the draft on **7 Oct 1941**, listing his mother Roxie Long as next of kin. Rather than wait to be called back to serve in the infantry, William walked into a Navy recruiting station in San Diego seven days later and on **14 Oct 1941** he signed up to serve in the US Navy.

### III. The Perichoretic Anchor: Seventy-one Days on Patrol Four

On **10 Dec 1942**, while _Silversides_ was undergoing refit at Brisbane, William Rae stepped across the gangway and entered the torpedo rooms alongside George Hollis Jr.

Seven days later, on **17 Dec 1942**, the ROC on sailing confirms that both Hollis and Rae went to sea together as Torpedomen. Hollis having already struck for the rating, and Rae learning the ropes as a Seaman second class, both set out on the [[silversides-patrol-04|Fourth Patrol]]. These men would share the single most famous torpedo-room crisis in _Silversides_ history. Over forty-five days between Brisbane and Pearl Harbor, Hollis and Rae were present for Pharmacist's Mate Thomas Moore's emergency appendectomy on the wardroom table, the sinking of the _Toei Maru_, and the harrowing ordeal off Truk on **20 Jan 1943**. 

After firing a spread at a Japanese convoy, a live, armed Mark XIV torpedo was stuck halfway out of the number 6 forward torpedo tube. As Japanese escorts pummeled the hull with depth charges, a forward gasket blew, flooding the forward torpedo room and forcing the sub down to a crushing **420 feet**. Hollis and Rae were part of the crew that bailed water by hand and held the ship together until LCDR Creed Burlingame made the call to refire the stuck torpedo so that the outer door could be cranked shut.

### IV. Diverging Wakes: The East China Sea and the Cape Fear River

Nineteen days after _Silversides_ brought her battered crew safely back to Pearl Harbor, Hollis and Rae parted ways.

On **19 Feb 1943**, **George Hollis** transferred off _Silversides_ to the submarine tender **USS _Sperry_ (AS-12)**. He served out the remainder of the war, advancing to **Torpedoman's Mate First Class (TM1c)** before receiving an honorable discharge in 1946. After six years in the Navy, Hollis returned home to his parent's hoome in Leland, NC. By the 1950 census, he was working locally as an attendant dispensing gas at a service station while his father remained at the fertilizer plant. Before long, George Jr. joined his father at the **Armour Fertilizer Works** along the Cape Fear River. He never married. On **7 Oct 1960**, the anniversary of his registration for the draft and at just **thirty-nine years old**, the veteran of _Trigger_ and _Silversides_ died of a sudden heart attack. His father, George Sr., served as the informant on his death certificate, and George Jr. was laid to rest in Wilmington National Cemetery (Section 5, Site 1476). 

**William Hatfield Rae** stayed aboard _Silversides_ through patrols five and six until **27 Sep 1943**, earning his silver dolphins `TM3c(SS)`, before heading to Groton, CT for new construction submarines. It was there that he commissioned the _Balao_-class submarine **USS _Caiman_ (SS-323)** on **17 Jul 1944** and married **Evelyn A. Rae** of nearby Providence, RI. By February 1945, Rae was back in the Pacific assigned to Submarine Division 282 at Guam. On **22 Feb 1945**—seventeen days before she sailed for her twelfth and final patrol—Rae was transferred to the **USS _Trigger_ (SS-237)**, the ship that gave _Silversides_ George Hollis Jr and the mislabeled flood valve three years earlier.

In late March of 1945, _Trigger_ was lost with all 89 hands in the Nansei Shoto. Today, when a vistor stands in front of the _Trigger_ flood valve in Muskegon, or steps inside _Silversides_' forward torpedo room looking at tube no. 6, they are standing in a space shared for 71 days by the man from North Carolina who brought _Trigger_ into the world and the man from Reno who went down with her in the end.
