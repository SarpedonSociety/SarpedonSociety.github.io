## Prewar (1914–1941)
Elroy Wilke was born on 25 June 1914. In 1930 he lived in Union Township, Iowa, working as a farm helper on the family farm. He enlisted in the Navy on 28 September 1937, at twenty-three. The Iowa benefits application puts his first foreign service from 16 September 1940 to 7 October 1941, and *Swordfish* appears among his boats.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Sep 1942)
Wilke commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as MM2c and made her first two war patrols under C.C. Burlingame. On 25 September 1942, as a MoMM1c, he was transferred to ComSubDiv 102 "for duty," the date of the boat's mass transfer to the flag allowance that included [[SRS-007|Walter J. Czerwinski]] and [[SRS-015|Michael L. Esposito]]. The remarks note that he and his brother left the ship on the same date.

## Later War (1943–1945)
He was a plankowner of USS *Tinosa* (SS-283) when she commissioned on 15 January 1943, and left on 1 January 1944 for the *Puffer* flag allowance, which in the same month took [[SRS-020|Glenn K. Hickman]] from *Tinosa* to *Bluefish*. The Iowa benefits application gives overseas service from 15 December 1941 to 17 March 1944, when he left *Puffer* for Mare Island. He came home until the end of the war, and returned to *Tinosa* as a CMoMM on 15 September 1945.

## Postwar (1945–1997)
The Iowa application gives a second overseas span from 15 September 1945 to 11 September 1947. The 1950 census finds him in Arnolds Park, Iowa, a Chief Engineman in the Navy. He retired after twenty years of service, and a photograph records him at his retirement ceremony as a Chief Engineman at the U.S. Navy Recruiting Station in Minneapolis, inspecting the crew. He later became president of the Submarine Veterans of World War II and wrote from Denver, Colorado, seeking veterans of the submarine service. BIRLS gives his discharge as 15 April 1957, and he died on 21 December 1997.
