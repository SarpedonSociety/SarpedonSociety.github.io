## Prewar (1924–1941)
Harold Schwartz was born on 7 June 1924. In 1930 he lived in a $20 rental in the village of Dover, Ohio, with his father William (33), a golf-course laborer, and his mother Marie (22). By 1940 he and his father were living on a $2,500 farm with William's brother and two sisters, and the census gives his age as 17, two years older than his birth date implies. With his parents' permission he enlisted in the Navy in June 1941, at seventeen.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Sep 1943)
Schwartz commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as Sea2c, six months after he enlisted. He made her first six war patrols, the first five under C.C. Burlingame and the sixth under J.S. Coye Jr., and left on 18 September 1943, six days after the sixth patrol ended. Robert Trumbull's *Silversides* places him as the second loader on the deck gun in a surface action when machine-gun fire knocked the helmet off his head. His obituary also credits him with a Silver Star, a Purple Heart and a Navy Commendation Medal with Combat V, none of which has yet been documented.

## Later War (1943–1945)
He became a torpedoman and commissioned USS *Hammerhead* (SS-364) as TM2c on 1 March 1944. He left her on 1 February 1945. His obituary says he made nine war patrols, six in *Silversides* and three in "Hardhead."

## Postwar (1945–2006)
Schwartz stayed in the Navy after the war and made Chief in 1963 after twenty-two years of service. The 1950 census finds him in Westlake, Ohio, in his aunt's household, listed as a Navy torpedoman. His wife was Vivian, his father was divorced and a golf-course greenskeeper, and Schwartz's home in 1949 was San Diego. He completed a 24-year naval career, then worked fifteen years at Electric Boat in Groton, Connecticut, in the shipboard and nuclear test organizations, retiring in 1981. He was a life member and past chief of the Center Groton Fire Department, a member of the Submarine Veterans of World War II and a Freemason at Brainard Lodge in Niantic. His Holland Club induction recognized his fifty years as a qualified submariner. He died on 19 February 2006.
