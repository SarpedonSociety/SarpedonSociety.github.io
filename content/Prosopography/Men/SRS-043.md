## Prewar (1905–1941)
Joe Von Teem was born on 17 October 1905 and is recorded as a native of Ellijay, Georgia. In 1920 his widowed mother Leeanna (36) ran a boarding house with six tenants at 151 Archer Street in Canton, Georgia, and Joe, called Von (14), lived there with his brothers Coy (15) and "Bub" (5). By 1930 his mother, now Lee A. Featherstone (46), had remarried and lived in Atlanta with Harry Featherstone (27), and Joe (24) was unemployed there with his brother Robert (16). He enlisted in the Navy on 18 December 1925, aged twenty, so he was a veteran signalman when the *Silversides* commissioned.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – 1942)
Teem commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as SM1c. On 25 March 1942 he was transferred to the U.S. Naval Hospital, Mare Island, for treatment, six days before the 31 March muster, and his absence from it was real and not a transcription gap. He was received back on 22 June 1942, the day after her first war patrol ended, and appears on the 30 June muster, which still carries his original 15 December 1941 received date.

He does not appear on the ROCs between June and September 1942, nor on the September muster, nor on the muster or ROCs for the *Silversides* flag allowance. He was out of the boat before the autumn, and whether he made her second patrol (15 July – 8 September 1942) is open.

## Later War (1942–1945)
He was a plankowner of the attack transport USS *Monrovia* (APA-31), commissioned 1 December 1942, and was transferred to the Amphibious Operating Base at Waipio on 27 February 1944. *Stingray* (SS-186) also appears among his boats. He rose to Chief Signalman (Permanent Appointment) and finished as a Chief Quartermaster.

## Postwar (1945–1962)
The 1950 census finds him in Hillsborough County, Florida, with his wife Alice (37) and two young daughters. He listed no employment and said he was not looking for work. He died on 21 February 1962 at the Maxwell Air Force Base hospital in Montgomery, Alabama, at fifty-six. His obituary in the *Tampa Tribune* of 23 February calls him a retired naval veteran of the Second World War and the Korean War, a member of Naval Reserve Local No. 118 and Iron Workers Local No. 397, and a resident of Tampa for seventeen years, at 2103 Temple Terrace Highway. His wife, three daughters, a brother and a granddaughter survived him.
