## Prewar (1917–1941)
Albert Houston Stegall, who went by Houston, was born in Kingston, Tennessee, on 10 March 1917. In 1930 the family owned a $3,500 home with a radio on Spring Street, where his father Albert H. (47) ran a peach orchard. Houston (12) was one of nine children at home. The father was a figure in Roane County: Justice of the Peace from 1918, a Mason and Methodist, elected Mayor of Kingston in June 1937, and the brother of the man for whom Clifford Stegall Post 110 of the American Legion is named. Houston enlisted in the Navy on 17 October 1935, at eighteen.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – May 1943)
Stegall commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as RM2c and made her first four war patrols, all under C.C. Burlingame. He left as a Chief Radioman (Acting) on 8 May 1943, before the fifth patrol sailed.

According to newspaper accounts, he held a bent spoon as a retractor in the wardroom while the boat's pharmacist's mate performed an appendectomy on a shipmate in Japanese waters, and the crew later watched a Japanese horse race from the surfaced boat off the shore. A Navy announcement of a Silver Star for "bravery aboard submarines on war patrol in action against the enemy" names him among three enlisted men, giving his residence as Kingston and his wife as Helene Ruth, of Ukiah, California.

## Later War (1943–1945)
His service after May 1943 has not yet been traced. A Tennessee newspaper reported him on a visit home to Kingston, recalling his experiences in the Pacific.

## Postwar (1945–2009)
The 1950 census finds him in Los Angeles, listed in the Armed Forces, with his wife Helene (33) and three young children. BIRLS gives his discharge as 30 June 1957. He died on 27 December 2009 and is buried in Arlington National Cemetery, where his headstone lists him as a Chief Warrant Officer 2 of World War II.
