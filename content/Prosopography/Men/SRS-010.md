## Prewar (1915–1941)
Lester De Muth was born on 9 June 1915. In 1930 the family rented a home at 140 Cedar Street in Boston for $37 a month. His father Harry (54), a packer at a furniture company, was born in New Jersey to French-born parents. His mother Margaret (48) was Canadian. Lester (14) lived there with his siblings Henry (19), Mildred (19) and Dorothy (12).

He enlisted in Boston on 27 May 1936, aged twenty. He was a plankowner of *Sailfish* (SS-192), the former *Squalus*, received on her commissioning day, 15 May 1940, from the Navy Yard, Portsmouth. He was advanced to MM1c on 1 August 1941 and left on 21 August for the *Snapper* flag allowance of CSD 22, together with [[SRS-009|Donald J. Davenport]], [[SRS-012|James F. Duckworth]] and [[SRS-016|Donald E. Finch]].

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Dec 1942)
De Muth commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as MM1c. He was appointed a temporary Machinist (warrant) on 1 November 1942, so he spent his last month aboard on the officer section of the muster. He made her first three war patrols under C.C. Burlingame and was transferred on 2 December 1942 to the Receiving Station, West Coast, for assignment to new construction.

## Later War and Postwar (1943–1959)
The boat he joined for new construction is not yet identified. After his appointment his service is on the officer and warrant registers, not the enlisted musters. He became a CWO4 on 9 June 1956 and retired on 1 May 1959.

He did not return to submarines. In April 1954 he was a CMACH (chief machinist) passenger in LST-1134, leaving Hilo on 5 April and Pearl Harbor on 13 April. In March 1957 he was a CWO4 at the Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard, and in November 1957 he was on temporary duty in LST-859. On 15 July 1958 he was on the officer roster of USS *Ticonderoga* (CVA-14) as a CWO4 in an auxiliaries material billet.

The 1950 census finds him in Vallejo, California, listed in the Armed Forces, with his wife Dorothy (35), who worked as a medical secretary at the hospital, and their one-year-old daughter Dianne, who was born in Hawaii. The 1950 Vallejo city directory has them at 331 Tinosa Avenue, a street named for a Gato-class submarine. He died on 15 December 1988; no obituary has been found.

## Research Notes
- **Departure:** the ROC transfer on 2 Dec 1942 is the authoritative endpoint.
- **Enlistment date:** the ROC gives 27 May 1936 and BIRLS gives 31 May 1936. Resolve from the OMPF.
- **New construction:** a detail formed from 2 Dec 1942 would have commissioned roughly Feb–Aug 1943. If he plankowned a second Gato as a warrant officer, an enlisted-SN match would never connect the two records. OMPF required.

  > **Silversides departure and the warrant appointment**
  >
  > - **Silversides ROC:** transferred **2 Dec 1942** to **RS West Coast for assignment
  >   to new construction**. This is the authoritative departure endpoint (not a
  >   last-muster-appearance inference).
  > - **Register of Chief Machinists and Machinists:** carries DeMuth as a **Machinist**
  >   (warrant) with **date of temporary rank 1 Nov 1942**. The number printed beside
  >   the name in that register is a **lineal / seniority ("signal") number**, not a
  >   service number.
  > - **What this register is:** a branch-specific warrant-officer lineal list — the
  >   same family of publication as the annual *Register of Commissioned and Warrant
  >   Officers of the U.S. Navy*, filtered to the warrant Machinist grade. Warrant
  >   officers are officers, so DeMuth's records from 1 Nov 1942 forward are in the
  >   officer/warrant registers, **not** the WWII enlisted muster roll collection —
  >   which is why the enlisted-muster search for his post-Silversides service comes up
  >   empty (same structural wall as **Ballman → Dorado**).
  > - **Career:** enlisted 1936 (SN 201-55-77) → temporary Machinist (warrant)
  >   1 Nov 1942 → **officer service number 204714** (officer series — no digit-overlap
  >   with the enlisted SN) → **CWO4 9 Jun 1956** → retired 1 May 1959 (BIRLS). A full
  >   mustang / warrant career.
  > - **He left Silversides already holding the warrant** (appointed 1 Nov 1942,
  >   detached 2 Dec 1942), so his final ~month aboard he would have been on the
  >   officer section of the muster.
  > - **Not a submariner after the war.** By the mid-1950s his billets are surface.
  >   **USS Ticonderoga (CVA-14):** on the ship's officer roster of **15 Jul 1958** as
  >   **CWO4**, billet listed as **"AUXILIARIEMATL"** — Engineering Department,
  >   **Auxiliaries (A) Division / material billet** (auxiliaries = pumps, hydraulics,
  >   refrigeration, A/C, steering and catapult/arresting-gear machinery, water systems;
  >   a textbook warrant-Machinist job). Other officers on the same roster carry billets
  >   like "Flight Deck" (V-1), "Electrical" (E Div.), "Photo", "Legal", "AVAORD"
  >   (aviation ordnance) — i.e. the roster is broken out by department/division billet,
  >   numbered columns for name / billet / rank / file number.
  >   Submarine service appears bounded by *Sailfish* → *Silversides* → the 1942-43
  >   new-construction detail; if he plankowned that boat it may be the only one he did
  >   as a warrant officer, or he was detached from the detail before commissioning
  >   (which would explain his total absence from every Gato commissioning roster).
