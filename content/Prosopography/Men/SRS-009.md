## Prewar (1916–1941)
Donald Davenport was born in Troy, New York, on 17 May 1916, the eldest of eight children. In 1930 the family owned a $2,125 home with a radio on Seventh Avenue in Watervliet, across the Hudson from Troy. His father Edwin (38), a Vermont-born former Marine, was a policeman on the guard force of the Watervliet Arsenal, where he served for twenty-seven years. Donald attended St. Bridget's School in Watervliet and Catholic Central High School, and worked at the Arsenal before he went to sea. His brother Edwin James Davenport also joined the Navy, in 1941, and later served as a Foreign Service officer.

Davenport enlisted on 18 July 1938 in Denver, Colorado. He was a Fireman First Class when USS *Sailfish* (SS-192), the salvaged *Squalus*, commissioned on 15 May 1940, and he came aboard that day as a plankowner. On 21 August 1941 he left for the *Snapper* flag allowance of CSD 22, the same transfer that took [[SRS-010|Lester J. De Muth]] and [[SRS-016|Donald E. Finch]]. The *Sailfish* commissioning roll also lists [[SRS-012|James F. Duckworth]] and [[SRS-013|Norris H. Duke]].

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Dec 1942)
Davenport commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as MM2c and made her first three war patrols under C.C. Burlingame, rising to MoMM1c. He left on 11 December 1942, six days before the fourth patrol sailed, for ComSubDiv 102 "for duty."

He should not be confused with Roy Milton Davenport, the boat's executive officer and later commanding officer of *Haddock* and *Trepang*, whose signature is on many of the *Silversides* reports of changes. The two are different men, and the museum's photo captions have mixed them up.

## Later War (1943)
His assignment between December 1942 and the commissioning of USS *Dorado* (SS-248) on 28 August 1943 is undocumented. The *Dorado* commissioning roll records him arriving from the Supervisor of Shipbuilding at Groton, Connecticut, as a CMoMM(AA). He was one of two chief motor machinist's mates received from that billet, along with a shipmate named Eberl.

## Death (12 October 1943)
Davenport was lost with *Dorado* on 12 October 1943, six weeks after she commissioned. He was twenty-seven. The California casualty register lists his wife, Rosaleen, of San Francisco, while the *Troy Record* of 23 October reported him missing to his parents. His father died on 30 March 1945 and is buried at St. Mary's Cemetery. Fellow *Dorado* plankowner [[SRS-002|August F. Ballman]] was lost in the same sinking.

## Research Notes
- **Newspaper claims (`acknowledged_speculation`):** the *Troy Record* credits him with Midway, Coral Sea and Guadalcanal, ten ships sunk including a 13,000-ton transport, and two looks at Japan through a periscope, once at Kobe harbor. *Silversides* reached Pearl Harbor on 4 Apr 1942, and her first patrol only spanned those battles. The Kobe detail is specific enough to chase. The paper also says he served on submarines "since 1935," which sits poorly against an enlistment of record in 1938.
- **Gap, Dec 1942 – Aug 1943:** detached "for duty," not new construction, unlike De Muth nine days earlier (2 Dec 1942). The *Troy Record* says "several other undersea boats," so another boat is possible.
- **Enlistment:** 18 Jul 1938 (two *Sailfish* ROCs) vs 29 Apr 1942 (Sep 1942 muster, 16 Dec 1942 ROC, *Dorado* commissioning ROC), which reads as a reenlistment.
- **Sailfish group:** the 15 May 1940 commissioning ROC (page 1) lists Davenport, De Muth, Duckworth, Duke and Finch. This documents the "five" in the De Muth and Duckworth notes. `n_commissionings` is 2 because *Sailfish* (SS-192) is Sargo class.
- **Continuity table:** the 1942-12 GAP flag was a false positive from the old generator, which counted the "Men Who Left Between Quarters" prose as presence. His last roster appearance is 30 Sep 1942.
