## Prewar (1916–1941)
Glenn Hickman was born on 22 August 1916. In 1930 the family owned a $500 home in Drumright, Oklahoma, where his father John (49) was a tool pusher in the oil fields. Glenn (13) had a brother John (16). He enlisted in the Navy on 15 May 1935, at eighteen. *Seawolf* and the old boat R-14 appear among his boats, the dates unrecorded.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Jul 1942)
Hickman commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as MM1c and made her first war patrol under C.C. Burlingame. On 10 July 1942, five days before the second patrol sailed, he was transferred to CSD 101 "for duty," the same day [[SRS-021|Elmer G. Huter]] and [[SRS-034|Richard C. Richardson Jr.]] left.

## Later War (1943–1945)
He was a plankowner of USS *Tinosa* (SS-283) when she commissioned on 15 January 1943, and he was advanced to CMoMM(AA) on 1 June 1943 under BuPers circular letter 38-43. On her third war patrol, on 3 October 1943, a test dive found a leak in the outboard ventilation supply system. After the boat fired four torpedoes on 4 October and lost depth control, Hickman volunteered at 0200 on 5 October to go into the superstructure in enemy waters to search for it. He found the loose hand-hole plate on the outboard supply valve and tightened it, which made the repair. The commanding officer commended him in the patrol report. The next day, 6 October, a target charged the boat, which went deep, and four close depth charges burst, breaking bridge speaker diaphragms, throwing men off their feet and starting a fire in the motor room. He left *Tinosa* on 1 January 1944 for the *Bluefish* flag allowance, in the same month [[SRS-045|Elroy F. Wilke]] went to *Puffer*.

## Postwar (1945–1962)
BIRLS, from his headstone application, gives his discharge as 17 January 1955, after nearly twenty years. A 1958 Fort Lauderdale city directory lists Glenn K. Hickman, engineer at the U.S. Ordnance Laboratory test facilities. He died on 29 July 1962.

  > 3 OCT 1943: On test dive discovered leak in supply ventilation system outside hull. System had been tight on previous dives. Unable to locate leak without undue risk at this time. 
  > 4 OCT 1943: 0455K Fired four torpedoes from bow tubes. Submerged to periscope depth. 0456K Lost depth control. Poppet of first tube fired stuck in open position. It was secured immediately after firing all tubes. This water, plus the flooding of the ventilation supply piping, made ship to heavy to control. 
  > 5 OCT 1943: 0200K Battery charge completed. Moon had set. Lay to and put air test on ventilation outboard piping. Glenn K. Hickman, CMoMM(AA) volunteered to enter superstructure and search for leak; finally located in hand hole plate of outboard ventilation supply valve, which was loose. Trouble appeared caused by poor rubber gasket material which had compressed under load and had lost its resilience. Tightening plate made satisfactory repair.
