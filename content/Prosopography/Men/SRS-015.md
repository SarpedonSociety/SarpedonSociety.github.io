## Prewar (1912–1941)
Michael Esposito was born on 26 April 1912. In 1930 the family rented a home in Newark, New Jersey, for $35 a month. His parents Antonio (45) and Michaelina (41) were born in Italy and arrived in 1904, and both were still aliens. Antonio was a baker who owned his own shop, and Michael (18) worked there as a baker, with his sisters Eva (20), Ida (16) and Emalia (11) at home. He was later a plankowner of the submarine *Sculpin*.

## On *Silversides* (Dec 1941 – Sep 1942)
Esposito commissioned aboard [[Silversides|*Silversides*]] on 15 December 1941 as MM1c. On his dates he made her first two war patrols under C.C. Burlingame. On 25 September 1942 he was transferred to the *Silversides* flag allowance.

## Later War (1942–1945)
He stayed on the flag allowance aboard USS *Sperry* until 10 February 1943, when he was routed through the Receiving Station, Pearl Harbor, and the West Coast to the Submarine Base, New London, for assignment to new construction by ComSubLant. He accepted an appointment as Ensign at the Philadelphia Receiving Station in September 1943, and his enlisted trail ends there. His later wartime postings have not been traced.

## Postwar (1945–1993)
BIRLS gives his discharge as 31 March 1958. The 1950 census finds him in Newark, listing his occupation as "NAVY," with his wife Carolina (35) and children Michael Jr. (6) and Margaret (4). His Find a Grave memorial lists him as a Lieutenant Commander, with the submarine dolphins on his headstone. He died on 2 June 1993.

## Research Notes
- **Departure:** 25 Sep 1942 is the flag-allowance transfer; the later *Sperry* months were a paper attachment.
- ***Flier*:** he was in the pipeline for USS *Flier* but survived the war, so he either was not assigned to her or was transferred off before her loss.
- **"NC"** in the 10 Feb 1943 transfer is read as new construction. `verify`
- ***Sculpin*:** one of four *Silversides* plankowners who came from her: [[SRS-005|Arthur W. Clark Jr.]], [[SRS-021|Elmer G. Huter]] and [[SRS-023|Peter J. Lewis]].
- **Open:** OMPF, his boat after September 1943.
